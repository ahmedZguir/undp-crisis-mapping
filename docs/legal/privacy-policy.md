# Privacy notice

This is a plain-language explanation of what happens to the information
you share when you report damage through this app. Please read it before
you continue. By tapping **"I agree and continue"** you confirm you have
read and understood this notice.

We have tried to keep this short and honest. If anything here is
unclear, you do not have to use the app.

## Who runs this app

This crisis-mapping platform is operated by **QCRI Humanitarian AI Group** as
part of a UNDP partner program, to help response teams understand where
damage has occurred and direct help to where it is needed.
**QCRI Humanitarian AI Group** is responsible for the information you share and
decides how it is used.


## You do not need an account

You do not give us your name, email address, or phone number. You do not
log in. The app creates a random identifier on your device so that *you*
can find and delete your own reports later. This identifier is not linked
to your identity, and you can reset it at any time in **Settings**.

## What we collect when you submit a report

When you choose to send a report, it includes:

- **A photo** you take or select. Photos can contain hidden technical
  information added by your camera or phone, such as the location and
  time the photo was taken. We store this technical information together
  with the photo.
- **The location** of the damage you are reporting (a point on the map).
- **What you type** — your description of the damage and, if you add it,
  notes on how to find the place.
- **Basic details about the damage** — for example how severe it is and
  the type of structure or infrastructure affected.
- **A random identifier** created on your device (described above).

We do **not** ask for, and you should **not** include, information that
identifies you or another person by name unless you choose to and
understand it will be visible to response coordinators.

## Why we collect it

To help response coordinators see where damage has occurred, group
related reports, and direct assistance. We use the information only for
crisis response and coordination, not for advertising and not for any
unrelated purpose.

## Who can see your report

- **Response coordinators** can see the full details of your report,
  including the photo, location, and your description. They are working
  on the crisis response.
- **What the public map shows depends on how the response team has configured this crisis.** By default the public sees only combined damage counts per area — never your individual report, your photo, or the exact location of any single report. Areas with very few reports are hidden so that no single report can be singled out.
- If the response team enables **building pins**, the public sees a pin for each damaged building showing its location and damage severity — no photo, no description, and no information that identifies you.
- If the response team enables **full reports**, the public can see individual reports including photos, descriptions, and locations. Your report and any photo you submitted will be visible to anyone. The app will tell you if the crisis you are reporting to has this setting before you submit.
- **You** can see your own reports on the map and in your history, on the
  device you submitted them from.

## A note about risk

If being identified as the person who reported damage could put you or
someone else at risk, please think carefully about what you photograph
and write. The public map is designed so that individual reports cannot
be looked up — but a photo or a description can still contain details
that identify a place or a person to someone who already knows the area.

## Your choices and rights

- **You can delete everything.** In **Settings**, "Delete my data"
  permanently removes every report and photo you have submitted from this
  device. This cannot be undone.
- **You can reset your identifier** in Settings, so future reports are
  not linked to your past ones.
- **You can stop at any time.** Using the app is voluntary.

## Keeping information safe

Information is sent over an encrypted connection and stored on secured
infrastructure. Access to full report details is limited to authenticated
response coordinators. No system is perfectly secure, and we do not
promise that it is — but we limit what we collect, limit who can see it,
and give you a way to delete it.

## How long we keep it

The most reliable way to remove your information is to delete it
yourself: "Delete my data" in **Settings** erases your reports and
photos immediately, at any time. Beyond that, we keep reports only for
as long as they are useful to the response for the related crisis. Once
that crisis response is closed, we automatically delete its reports,
photos, and any data derived from them 90 days later.

## Changes to this notice

If we change this notice in a way that affects you, the app will ask you
to read and agree again before you continue reporting.

## Questions

If you have questions about how your information is handled, contact the
response team through the channel they have shared with your community.
